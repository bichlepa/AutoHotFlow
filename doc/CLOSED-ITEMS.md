### F-0011: `Scheduler.Advance` pops before the state change

**What:** `Scheduler.Advance` calls `Pop` before `finished -> ready`. With an empty stack (M-0014) it throws instead of ending the thread with `exception`.
**Status:** Closed (2026-10-13), fixed in 183c561. Verified: the effect was worse than an error in the log. `Pop` threw out of `Scheduler.Advance`, out of `Executor._Advance` and `_OnFinish`, into the finish callback of the element; the thread stayed `finished` for ever and the instance never ended (read in code: `Scheduler.Advance`, `Executor._OnFinish`/`_Advance`). Reproduced by the new unit test `Loop: leaving with an empty stack ends the thread as unhandled (F-0011)` in `tests/ahk/engine_scheduler.test.ahk`: before the fix `error: AhfError: LoopStack.Pop: loop stack is empty`, after the fix 21 of 21 pass (direct `ahkrun.py` call, no pytest run id). Fix: with `leavesLoop` and an empty stack the thread is released, the outcome is `removed` with `unhandled` true, and the executor logs `f0` with the message `loop stack is empty when leaving loop ...`. This matches M-0014 ("thread ends with exception"). Not checked: the pytest fast tier and the engine end-to-end with such a flow (no flow that enters a loop at `tail` exists in the test data).
**Why deferred:** (was) low priority; the bad case needs an empty stack.
**Sign that it matters:** An unhandled error from `Advance` in the engine log when a stack is empty.
**Action:** Proposal from the source: `Advance` catches the error and ends the thread with `exception`.
**Evidence:** `v2-phase2-final:work_items/phase2b/WI-0021-engine-korrektheit.md`.

### F-0025: Child in handshake is not covered by the orphan guard

**What:** A child that is still in the handshake is not covered by the orphan guard. It can live up to 10 s after the supervisor has died.
**Status:** Accepted by the project owner (2026-10-13): low impact, the 10 s stay (option (b) below). Described in `doc/ARCHITECTURE.md` 6.3 point 4 (2026-10-14).
**Update 2026-10-13 (bug hunter):** Cause verified by reading and by one measurement. `Role._Guard` (`src/core/Role.ahk`) starts the orphan clock only when `peer.status = "reconnecting"`; for every other status it resets `lostSince`. A role that has never been welcomed is `connecting` (first attempts) or `handshake`, so the guard does not count. The role then ends by the budget of the peer, not by the guard: `Peer.DEFAULT_RECONNECT_TOTAL_MS` (10 s) for a hub that is not there, and `helloTimeoutMs` (3 s) per attempt plus 3 s guard when the hub accepts the pipe but does not answer. Measured: `python tools/ahkrun.py src/AutoHotFlow.ahk --role=worker --hub=<pipe nobody serves> ...` ends with exit 4 `peer closed (hub_unreachable)` after 10.2 s (direct call, no pytest run id, commit 4d97c49), against about 3 s after a connection that was already ready (`test_role_ends_with_exit_4_when_its_pipe_is_cut_and_the_hub_does_not_come_back`). A supervisor that dies while a child is still starting therefore leaves that child for up to 10 s, not 3 s. A hub that is dead but whose pipe broke after the welcome is covered. Not changed: the fix is in `src/core/` and needs the project owner's agreement.
**Options:** (a) In `Role._Guard` also count `connecting` and `handshake` as lost (clock from the start of the process), so ARCHITECTURE 6.3 point 4 holds without exception; risk: a role started by hand or by a test before its hub (the supervisor creates the hub pipe before it starts children, read, not checked for every test setup) would give up after 3 s instead of 10 s, so such tests must be checked. (b) Keep the 10 s and describe it in ARCHITECTURE 6.3 ("a role that has not yet connected waits up to 10 s"). (c) Lower `reconnectTotalMs` for the first connect only. Recommendation: (a) with a test that starts a role without a hub and expects exit 4 within `ORPHAN_BUDGET_S`; low priority, the effect is a stray process for a few seconds.
**Update 2026-10-14 (bug hunter), follow-up "hub accepts the pipe but never answers `sys.hello`":** The 10 s in the update above did not hold for this case. Reproduced (verified): a hub stand-in that accepts and reads but never answers; the role sent 9 `sys.hello` in 25 s and was still alive (test `test_hub_that_never_answers_hello_ends_the_role_with_exit_4`, run ID `20261014-064634-7512`, base commit 1e444c2). Cause: the hello timeout closes the connection, `Peer._BeginReconnect` restarted the 10 s budget at each failed hello, and the orphan guard resets its clock in `handshake`; so neither ended the role. Fixed: `_BeginReconnect` keeps the budget when the connection ended in `handshake`, so the budget counts from `Connect` or from the loss of a ready connection, as ARCHITECTURE 6.3 point 4 says. After the fix the role ends with exit 4 after 12.3 s (budget 10 s plus the running hello, 3 s at most); run ID `20261014-064717-1744`, `test_role.py` 11 passed; `test_peer.py` 10 passed (`20261014-064746-2828`).
**Why deferred:** Low priority.
**Sign that it matters:** A child process still runs up to 10 s after the supervisor is gone.
**Action:** Describe the behavior in `doc/ARCHITECTURE.md` 6.3, or close the gap.
**Evidence:** `v2-phase2-final:work_items/phase2b/WI-0027-worker-pool.md`.

### F-0026: Lost `sys.busy {busy: false}`

**What:** A lost `sys.busy {busy: false}` message leaves the worker shown as busy.
**Status:** Closed (2026-10-13) for the case that can be shown, fixed in 4d97c49, see the update. Before: assumed, low priority.
**Update 2026-10-13 (bug hunter):** Verified by reading and by a test. `Peer.Send` drops a message without a trace while the peer is not `ready` (`droppedSends`, `src/core/ipc/Peer.ahk`). `Worker.OnReady` announced the busy state only for a running job (`true`), never `false`. If the link broke during a job and the job ended in the gap, `sys.busy {busy:false}` was dropped, the reconnect announced nothing, and the supervisor kept the worker as busy for ever: `Children.OnLinkLost` resets nothing, `OnHello` ignores a child that is not `starting` and `Heartbeat.Tick` skips a busy child. Effect: an idle worker that later hangs is never detected, and the process is not ended as hung. The engine's 5 s acceptance deadline does not cover this (it concerns the job, not the heartbeat). Fix: `Worker.OnReady` always announces the current state (`true` or `false`). The new test `test_worker_announces_its_busy_state_again_after_it_rebuilt_the_connection` (`tests/fault/test_pipe_break.py`, stand-in hub, link cut by the hub) fails before (`['hello', 'hello']`, run 20261013-183502-13236) and passes after (run 20261013-183508-7492, commit 4d97c49 plus the test). `test_worker.py` and `test_architecture.py` 44 passed (run 20261013-183521-20328). Decision taken: fix in the worker, not in the supervisor; the alternative (reset `busy` when the child's link is lost or on a new hello) would also cover a worker that dies in the gap, but a worker that is still running a job would then be pinged until its `true` arrives. Not checked: the exact race (job end inside the gap) is not forced, the test checks the announcement after a reconnect; the engine does not receive the lost `evt.worker.done` either (a separate matter: the slot is released by the engine's own deadlines, read in `WorkerBridge`, not tested).
**Why deferred:** (was) The engine's acceptance deadline of 5 s was thought to cover the case; it does not.
**Sign that it matters:** A worker shown as busy for more than 5 s.
**Action:** Verify the case if it is seen. Otherwise no action.
**Evidence:** `v2-phase2-final:work_items/phase2b/WI-0027-worker-pool.md`.

### F-0031: Hub connection deadlines ignore supervisor stalls

**What:** Two time-bound spots in the hub connection do not know about a supervisor stall: `PipeConnection.WRITE_GONE_MAX_MS` and the drain deadline of `CloseWhenFlushed`. Both run on the wall clock.
**Status:** Closed as not a defect (see the update below).
**Why deferred:** Low priority. The effect would be that the connection of a process that is already gone is closed after a resume without the rest of its data (`peer_closed`).
**Sign that it matters:** A connection closed with `peer_closed` after a stall, with data missing.
**Action:** Apply the same stall handling as in WI-0033 (`Shift` of the deadline) to these two spots. The change is in `src/core/ipc/` and needs the project owner's agreement.
**Update 2026-10-15 (bug hunter):** Read in code and checked with a test; the feared effect does not occur. (1) `WRITE_GONE_MAX_MS`: `PipeConnection.Pump` reads first (`_PumpRead`) and only then judges the age of `_writeGoneAt`. After a stall the first Pump therefore delivers what lies in the pipe, and the connection ends with `peer_closed`, the same reason the end of the stream gives. What lies in the pipe is at most `PipeListener.BUFFER_SIZE` (64 KiB, one read; the limit of 64 reads per Pump is far away). Frames the peer still had in its own queue never reached the pipe, and no deadline changes that. Verified by `tests/ahk/pipe_write_gone.test.ahk` (stale `_writeGoneAt`, 5 s old: the frame is delivered, then `peer_closed`; passes on `1b0831d`, run by `ahkrun.py`, exit 0). (2) Drain deadline of `CloseWhenFlushed`: only `Peer.CloseWhenFlushed`, called from `Role.ahk`, uses it; the supervisor and its hub connections never do (`grep` over `src/`). A role is not stalled by the supervisor's stall, so the supervisor's `Shift` has nothing to move there; a stall of the role itself shortens the wait for a process that is ending anyway. **Proposal:** close as not a defect (no `Shift` needed); the test stays as a regression test. Waits for the project owner's confirmation because the item was filed with the wish to change `src/core/ipc/`.
**Evidence:** `v2-phase2-final:work_items/phase2b/WI-0033-worker-und-stillstand.md`.

**Closed 2026-10-15.** Owner decision (option A): not a defect. The test `tests/ahk/pipe_write_gone.test.ahk` stays as a regression test; no change to `src/core/ipc/`.

### F-0039: Slow child during shutdown holds later groups (phase 3, decision needed)

**What:** A child that starts during shutdown, when its group is reached, holds that group and all later groups until its `sys.hello`. If it starts slower than the deadline (for example a worker under load), `storage` is killed at the deadline without having received `sys.shutdown`. The symptom is like F-0037, but rarer.
**Status:** Read in code, not reproduced.
**Why deferred:** The fix changes the order of the shutdown ranks, so the project owner must decide.
**Sign that it matters:** `storage` is force-stopped at the deadline without `sys.shutdown`, under load with workers.
**Action:** Proposal from the source: a child that has never connected does not count as a condition for its group, because it has sent nothing that `storage` must write. The project owner decides; then implement and test.
**Evidence:** `v2-phase2-final:work_items/phase2b/WI-0034-abnahmefunde.md` (section "Ergebnis").
**Update 2026-10-13 (bug hunter), explanation and recommendation. Status stays: decision needed.**
**What happens, in plain words.** The supervisor ends the session group by group, by rank: 0 UI (manager, editor, debug), 1 engine, 2 triggerhost, 3 everything else (workers), 4 storage (`Children._RankOf`, `ShutdownGroups`). Storage is last on purpose: it must still be there while the others hand over their data. A group counts as done only when each of its children has said `sys.bye` or has ended (`Children.GroupDone`: `!bye && !gone` blocks). A child that was started shortly before the exit and has not yet said `sys.hello` (state `starting`) is alive, so it is in its group (`ShutdownGroups` takes everything that is not `gone`). It cannot be reached (`SendShutdown` only sends; it has no link), so it cannot say `bye`, and its group stays open. The groups behind it, in the worst case `storage`, are never started (`Shutdown.Tick`: the loop only goes on when `GroupDone`). If the child's hello comes in time, `Shutdown.OnHello` (the F-0037 fix) sends it `sys.shutdown` and everything goes on; the exit is only delayed. If the hello comes after the deadline, `Shutdown._Force` terminates all living children, `storage` among them, which never got `sys.shutdown`. Exit code 5, and `storage` is `clean: false`.
**When and how it would show.** Only when the exit starts while a child is still starting, and the child needs longer than the deadline (default 5 s) for its hello. Candidates: the warm worker after a start or after a worker was ended, an editor just opened, the debug window; on a slow or loaded machine (start is about 30 ms when idle, `doc/MEASUREMENTS.md`, so it needs heavy load or a deliberate delay). Signs: supervisor log `shutdown: deadline reached, terminating the remaining processes` followed by `terminating storage`, exit code 5, `evt.process.exited` of `storage` with `clean: false`, no `sys.shutdown` of `storage` in its log. Likelihood: low (a few hundred ms window before the exit multiplied by a rare slow start); it becomes more likely with phase 3, which has more roles that start on demand (editors, workers, trigger host). Cost: not a crash. The session still ends, but at the full deadline and with exit 5. Data loss is possible only if `storage` still held unwritten data (variable values, flow saves queued by the engine or the editor at their own `sys.shutdown`): a kill of `storage` without `sys.shutdown` skips its "empty the queue" step (ARCHITECTURE 6.3, point 2; `storage` code not read for this).
**Verified by a test run.** The case is reproduced with the existing test role `echo:slowhello<ms>` (rank 3, as a worker) in the scenario of `test_a_child_that_says_hello_during_the_shutdown_gets_sys_shutdown_and_the_session_exits_clean` (`tests/fault/test_shutdown.py`), with `slowhello4000` and a deadline of 1500 ms instead of 12000 ms (throwaway test, not committed; direct run, no run id in the report, run log `20261013-185737-22564`, commit cc1b7c8). Result: exit code 5 after 1.87 s, supervisor log `exit requested ... deadline 1500 ms`, then only `deadline reached, terminating the remaining processes`, `terminating echo:slowhello4000`, `terminating storage`, `client 'storage' gone (peer_closed)`; `evt.process.exited` of `storage`: `clean: false`, `exitCode: 1`. The log has no `shutdown: ... connected during the shutdown` line for storage and storage never got `sys.shutdown`. Assumed, not checked: that real workers behave like the test role (same rank, same state `starting`); the data-loss case with a busy `storage`; the effect of a restart of a crashed child (restarts are cancelled by `Children.BeginShutdown`, so a restarted child in `starting` is only possible if it was relaunched before the exit began).
**Recommendation (one):** The proposal from the source, in this form: `Children.GroupDone` does not wait for a child whose state is `starting` (it has never connected, so it has sent nothing that a later group must write). The path of its late hello stays as it is: `Shutdown.OnHello` sends it `sys.shutdown` (its group index is behind `groupIndex`, so it gets it at once, with the rest of the deadline), it says `bye` and ends. Reasons: (1) three lines in one function, no new state, the F-0037 path is already tested; (2) the ranks stay as they are, `storage` is still last for every child that is connected, which is the only kind that can have sent data; (3) a slow starter can no longer cost `storage` its `sys.shutdown`. ### F-0070: A shutdown with more than 1000 unwritten variables loses the rest

**What:** `VarWriter.FlushAll` (WI-0041) sends every marked variable as its own `cmd.storage.var.set` and ignores `MAX_INFLIGHT`. `Peer.Request` refuses a request when `Peer.MAX_PENDING` (1000) requests are already waiting (code `busy`). `_Send` then calls `_Failed` without a retry (the process ends), so every variable beyond the limit is not written; each one raises its own `evt.storage.error` and log line.
**Status:** verified in a probe (AHK unit level, stand-in peer that refuses after 1000 requests): 1100 marked variables, 1000 sent, 100 errors. Not reproduced with the real engine. It needs more than 1000 variables changed within the write delay of 200 ms, or more than 1000 variables marked again after a long storage outage; both are unlikely for the v1 scope (flows with some dozens of variables).
**Proposal:** if it ever matters, send the remainder in one request that carries several variables (a protocol change in `cmd.storage.var.set`), or report one summary line instead of one error per variable. No change made.

Requests of other users of the peer (for example unanswered flow writes) count against the same limit, so the real margin is below 1000. Without a protocol change nothing can be sent beyond the limit at shutdown, because waiting for answers would need a message loop on the shutdown path.
**Options (decision needed):** (a) a batch message `cmd.storage.var.set` with a list of variables (protocol change, storage and doc/PROTOCOL.md; the only option that loses nothing); (b) keep it and accept the loss, but report one summary line (count of variables lost) instead of one error each (small, no protocol change); (c) do nothing, the case is far outside the v1 scope. Recommendation: (b) now, (a) only if a flow with thousands of permanent variables appears. Not implemented because the data loss stays in (b) and (a) is a protocol change the owner should decide.
**Evidence:** probe described above; code `src/engine/vars/VarWriter.ahk` (`FlushAll`, `_Send`), `src/core/ipc/Peer.ahk` (`Request`).
**Resolution:** accepted by the project owner, option (b). Commit `a62d17d` ("F-0070: report one summary line when a shutdown flush loses variables"): `FlushAll` counts the requests the peer refuses and logs one line, `shutdown: <lost> of <total> variables not written (storage refused the requests)`, instead of one storage error per variable. ### F-0093: `WorkerBridge`: leftovers of a failed spawn, no start timeout for a slot in "starting"

### F-0012: Unprotected check-then-act spots in the engine

**What:** Further spots that check and then change state without protection: `Executor._OnFinish`/`_StopRun`, `_ElementStarted`/`_ElementStopped`, and `WorkerBridge`.
**Status:** Open. Two spots fixed and verified by test (`WorkerBridge` job table, and the `Executor` active-thread map, 2026-10-16). `_ElementStarted`/`_ElementStopped` are guarded (`Critical`, ad6ac7d) but have no test, so they count as fixed, not verified. Not closed for that reason; the stress runs are not checked.
**Update 2026-10-13 (bug hunter):** Re-read `Executor` and `WorkerBridge` (verified by reading, nothing reproduced). Already protected: `_Advance`, `_DispatchWorker` (register, run, note execId), `_EndRun`, the tick flag, `ThreadState.Dispatch/Finish` (`Critical`). `_OnFinish` and `_StopRun` are safe against a late finish: `_StopRun` puts the threads into `stopping` first, and a `Finish` on such a thread answers `late`, so `_ElementStopped` is not called twice. `WorkerBridge` has no `Critical`, but its callbacks change only the slot list and the job map in short sequences with no sends between the check and the change; `Run` sets slot and job before it sends. One real spot found: `_ElementStarted` and `_ElementStopped` read, changed and stored `_counts` in separate lines; an interrupting dispatch tick for the same element could lose an update, so the count (and the `running` state shown to the UI) would be wrong for ever. Guarded with `Critical` in ad6ac7d. No test: the interruption cannot be forced. `WorkerBridge` stays unprotected on purpose; not proven to be needed. Not checked: stress runs.
**Update 2026-10-16 (bug hunter, verified):** `WorkerBridge` took a job with `Get`, then removed it with `Delete` in a second line, in four places (`_ForgetJob`, `_Finish`, `OnDone`, `Cancel`). An interrupting deadline timer (`_JobExpired`) could run between the two lines of an answer: `onDone` then ran twice, or the `Delete` threw `Item has no value`. Reproduced without a fix: a probe with a 1 ms deadline and direct answers ran 199,458 jobs with 5 double callbacks and 11 throws (second run 142,967 jobs, 9 and 3). Fixed: `WorkerBridge._TakeJob` takes the record with one `Delete` (a builtin call is not interrupted), and every caller acts only on the record it got. Test `AnswerAndDeadlineOfOneJobEndTheJobOnce` (`tests/ahk/engine_worker_bridge.test.ahk`) puts the deadline into the first `Get` of the job (a Map subclass): red before (`expected 1 but got 2`), green after. The probe after the fix: 234,140 jobs, 0 doubles, 0 throws. Fast tier: green except F-0102. Consequence before the fix (not checked in a role): a second `onDone` (`ExecContext.Finish` ignores it, logged as `second Finish ignored`), and an error in the message handler.
**Update 2026-10-16 (bug hunter, verified), `Executor` active-thread map:** `_OnFinish` checked `runRec["active"].Has(threadId)` and then `Delete`d it; `_StopRun` did `Has`, then `[threadId]`, then `Delete`. A finish of the thread that interrupts a stop (or the other way round) made the later access throw. Reproduced without a fix: a test that runs the other path just before the take (a Map subclass `SpyActive`, `Delete` override) gave `Item has no value.` in both orders: `StopFlow` raised (the stop was cut short; the loop does not reach the other threads and `_CheckEnded`; the instance staying `stopping` follows from the code, assumed, not shown), and `ExecContext.Finish` raised after the thread was already released. Fixed: both places take the entry with one `Delete` in a `try`, as `WorkerBridge._TakeJob` does. A `_StopRun` that loses the take does not stop the element (a finish has ended it) but still releases the thread; `_OnFinish` that loses the take answers `late` (`ThreadState.Finish` answers `late` for any thread that is not running). Tests `AStopThatMeetsTheFinishOfItsThreadCompletes` and `AFinishThatMeetsAStopOfItsThreadCompletes` (`tests/ahk/engine_executor.test.ahk`): red before (`Item has no value.`), green after; fast tier green. Not checked: stress runs. Open question for the owner: none in this spot.
